# Bounded retrieval adaptation

The evolution store is a separate SQLite database. It records only an opaque run ID, the recipe version selected before the run, a classified outcome, and optional structured feedback. It retains at most 1,000 runs and 10,000 recent run-ID tombstones to suppress duplicate observations after run eviction; version and audit records are retained. Duplicate suppression is bounded: an ID can be accepted again after its tombstone expires. No prompts, source text, or free-form feedback are stored as instructions. Feedback can be revised for a retained run. Do not expose internal recording operations directly to untrusted clients.

The active recipe adds zero, one, or two fixed Vietnamese retrieval hints about identity and evidence. It does not change provider configuration, budgets, source policy, evidence acceptance, risk rules, evaluator code, or application code. Source and user content remains untrusted. This module performs no network requests or model calls. A caller captures the snapshot before starting a request, applies its active recipe to the query, and attributes the eventual outcome to the captured active and shadow IDs. The normal provider request and budget rules still apply.

An evolution tick examines only outcomes for the current active version. Six retained runs and at least three distinct runs indicating the same relevant failure (outcome or structured feedback) are needed. Source failures and generic unhelpful feedback do not propose a change. A candidate can only add an allowlisted hint. A frozen eight-case synthetic replay compares the candidate against its parent, requiring higher cue coverage and no per-case regression; candidates remain shadow until explicitly promoted. This replay measures presence of retrieval cues, not answer quality, safety, model accuracy, or performance on real data. Shadow opportunities count runs that began while a shadow existed; no shadow model request is executed. The replay does not establish that a hint improves real retrieval.

Promotion checks that the candidate is still the current shadow, its parent is still active, the sample and triggering failure counts remain sufficient, and replay still passes. A tick retires a shadow if revised feedback or retention removes its support. A rollback may select only a previously active version. Creation, retirement, promotion, and rollback are audited; recipe versions are immutable. SQLite changes to active/shadow pointers and their audit events are transactional. Existing in-flight results are attributed to the recipe captured when they started. An empty or undersized sample leaves the active recipe unchanged.

Rollback also persists a quarantine for each hint added along the reverted version ancestry. The same hint cannot be proposed again from that parent until three new retained runs recorded after the rollback indicate that failure type. Changing feedback on older runs does not clear the quarantine, and adding another hint cannot bypass it. A subsequent rollback resets the watermark. If new feedback is revised or its runs expire before promotion, the gate closes again. Quarantine, the rollback event, and clearing the shadow are committed together and survive restart.

The synthetic fixtures are deliberately separate from the observed run records, but they are handcrafted and small. Their deterministic score can favor extra cues and cannot validate a model or source. Evaluate real retrieval quality separately before relying on a promoted version, and use rollback if real results degrade.
