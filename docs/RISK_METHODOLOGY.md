# Evidence-based enterprise screening (screening-v1)

This is a transparent analyst-led triage tool. It does not estimate default probability, establish allegations as fact, calculate a credit rating, or provide investment advice. An absence of records or concerning claims is not evidence of safety. Coverage depends on what has actually been collected, and the source, subject match, analyst, reasoning and dates should be inspected before making decisions.

## Inputs and review

A human reviews whether each source concerns the specific company using the existing evidence review. `subject_confirmed` only identifies the subject; it does not establish the truth of a claim. A human then records a separate structured assessment for legal, tax, financial or operations with a severity from 0 to 3 and a written rationale. The source's dimension must match the assessment. Levels are ordinal: 0 means the analyst found no concern in that source, 1 low, 2 moderate, 3 high. They are judgment categories, not probabilities. No keyword extraction or automated assertion verifies claims or generates these levels.

Entity identity is a separate, explicit human confirmation citing subject-reviewed evidence and an explanation of how the entity was distinguished. Candidate names, tax-ID syntax, search matches, and subject confirmation do not themselves confirm entity identity. An identity review can also withdraw confirmation. Each assessment, identity decision and assessment retraction is an append-only journal event with the analyst, reason and recording time. A corrected assessment is a new event; retract the old one when it should no longer contribute. Only assessments can be retracted; use a new identity decision for identity changes.

A new subject review invalidates prior decisions and assessments citing that source, including if the subject is subsequently reconfirmed. An analyst must assess again. A later identity decision replaces earlier ones even if its source becomes invalid; an older confirmation is never silently restored. Existing snapshots retain the full source and review references used at creation. Analysts should compare source identifiers and verify factual support, including the distinction between an allegation and an adjudicated finding; a structured entry cannot guarantee either.

## Coverage, scoring and ordering

The five screening dimensions are identity, legal, tax, financial and operations. Identity is an eligibility gate, not a scored concern. To receive a comparable score, identity must have an active confirmation and every other dimension must have at least one active, eligible assessment. Coverage reports the four assessed dimensions separately, alongside identity status. Missing, stale, rejected, re-reviewed, or retracted evidence is unknown and cannot become a zero. Each dimension uses the maximum severity among its active assessments, and the overall score uses the maximum across the four dimensions. This cautious rule prevents an adverse finding from being averaged away, but may overstate concern when evidence conflicts; analysts must resolve disagreement through documented review and retraction. No prescribed weighting or statistical calibration is implied.

Eligible companies are ordered by ascending overall concern; equal scores share competition rank (e.g. 1, 1, 3), with company ID used only for deterministic display order. Companies that fail any gate follow all scored companies, have no score or rank, and are not ordered by implied risk. Ranking is strictly within the explicitly selected cohort of 1–1,000 unique companies; it is not a market-wide league table. The selected cohort is scored and ordered in full before display pagination. When a catalogue exceeds the cohort limit, the selection is truncated and the interface must say so. Coverage is not a risk score.

## Time, freshness and limitations

Only source observations, known publication dates, subject reviews, analyst entries and retractions available at or before the requested system-known cutoff can contribute. A missing publication date is ineligible for scoring. Both publication and observation must be no older than 365 days at cutoff, a conservative fixed screening policy rather than a claim that all information expires on day 366. Date-only values are interpreted at the end of that date in Vietnam (UTC+7), avoiding an assumption that an event was known earlier that day. A date-only cutoff is the end of that day; if today is selected it is clamped to the time of calculation. Future cutoffs are rejected. The system does not reconstruct the historical state of the world, verify when a source actually became public, or recover information that was never observed. Journal recording times are assigned by the server, so a new assessment cannot be backdated into a historic screen.

A preview calculates without saving a snapshot. An explicit snapshot operation stores an immutable copy with methodology version, cutoff, creation time, cohort results and contributing source/assessment/identity provenance. A saved snapshot remains a record of that calculation even when later reviews, retractions or new evidence change a subsequent preview. It is not an authenticated document or proof that the sources were true. Subject-review history and the journal remain separately available for audit; application-level append-only behavior does not protect against direct database tampering.


Snapshot company names are taken from the current stored company record at calculation time and frozen in the snapshot; they are not a reconstruction of names at a historical cutoff. Date-only sources published today can be recorded and assessed immediately, but cannot contribute to a score until that day has ended in Vietnam.
